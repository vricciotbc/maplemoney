Build Your Life · Ages 18–30

Welcome to Maple Money

You are 18 years old with your whole financial life ahead of you. Every month you make real decisions — where to live, what to invest, how to handle unexpected costs. Your goal: build the strongest financial foundation you can by age 30.

📅
Each Month

You earn income, pay expenses, and choose where to put what's left. Income minus expenses = your monthly surplus. You can invest any amount up to your full cash balance.

📈
Your Investments Grow

Money in your HISA, TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA earns returns every month. The earlier you invest, the more time compound growth has to work. Even small amounts add up significantly over 12 years.

⚡
Life Happens

Random events fire each month — a car repair, a bonus, a medical bill. Your Emergency Fund can absorb shocks so they don't derail your plan. Without one, setbacks hit your cash directly.

🏠
Big Decisions

After graduation you'll choose a job offer. Later you'll decide whether to rent or buy a home. These choices have lasting financial consequences — there is no single right answer.

📊
Track Everything

Your net worth chart updates every month. Open the Portfolio tab anytime to see your investments broken down by account and asset type, including what you contributed vs. what grew.

⚠️
Watch for Limits

The TFSA has a $7,000/year contribution limit. The FHSA has an $8,000/year and $40,000 lifetime cap. Exceeding these in real life triggers CRA penalties. The game enforces them too.

Your Accounts Explained

🏦
HISA — High Interest Savings Account 4.5% APR · Fully liquid

The first thing every Canadian should set up. Your HISA earns 4.5% interest on every dollar — that's money working for you without any risk. Unlike investments, your cash here is always accessible and never loses value. In 2024 the best Canadian HISAs (EQ Bank, Oaken, Saven) pay 4–5%. Park your emergency fund and short-term savings here.

🛡️
Emergency Fund Target: 3 months of expenses

Not an account type — a savings goal. Financial experts recommend keeping 3–6 months of living expenses set aside before investing anything else. In the game, your Emergency Fund absorbs negative life events so they don't drain your cash. Without it, a $500 car repair hits your investing budget. With it, you never skip a beat.

🌿
TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account $7,000/yr limit · Tax-free growth & withdrawals

Canada's most flexible registered account. Every dollar you earn inside a TFSA — whether from interest, dividends, or capital gains — is completely tax-free. You can withdraw anytime for any reason and your contribution room comes back the following year. The annual limit in 2024 is $7,000. This is almost always the first account to max out.

🏔️
RRSP — Registered Retirement Savings Plan 18% of earned income · Tax deduction on contributions

Contributions reduce your taxable income — so if you earn $60,000 and contribute $5,000, you only pay tax on $55,000. Growth inside is tax-sheltered. You pay tax when you withdraw (ideally in retirement when your income is lower). Best used when you're in a higher tax bracket. Unlike a TFSA, withdrawn room is not restored.

🏠
FHSA — First Home Savings Account $8,000/yr · $40,000 lifetime · Tax-free for first home

A newer Canadian account combining the best of TFSA and RRSP for first-time buyers. Contributions are tax-deductible like an RRSP. Withdrawals for a qualifying first home purchase are completely tax-free like a TFSA. If you ever plan to buy a home, this account should be a priority — the tax benefits are exceptional.

Key Financial Concepts

Net Worth

Everything you own (assets) minus everything you owe (liabilities). Your cash, investments, and home equity are assets. Student debt, mortgage, and credit card balances are liabilities. Growing net worth is the goal.

Compound Growth

Earning returns on your returns. If you invest $1,000 and earn 8%, you have $1,080. Next year you earn 8% on $1,080, not $1,000. Over decades this becomes exponential. Starting at 18 vs. 28 makes a dramatic difference.

OSAP

Ontario Student Assistance Program. Provides grants (free money) and loans to eligible students. Grants never need repayment. Loans accumulate at 5% annual interest after graduation. Apply early every year.

Investment Risk vs Return

Index funds track the whole market — steady 6–10% average returns with low fees. Stocks pick individual companies — higher potential, higher volatility. Crypto is speculative — massive swings in both directions. Match your risk to your timeline and tolerance.

Amortization

How long it takes to fully pay off a mortgage. A 25-year amortization means 25 years of monthly payments. Shorter amortization = higher payments but less total interest. Longer = lower payments but you pay more in the long run.

Credit Score

A number from 300–900 that represents your creditworthiness. Above 750 is excellent. Paying bills on time, keeping credit card balances low, and having a long history all help. Affects mortgage rates, rental applications, and loan approvals.

Mortgage

A loan used to buy a home. You pay a down payment upfront (minimum 5% in Canada) and the bank covers the rest. Monthly payments cover interest + principal. As you pay down the mortgage and the home appreciates, your equity grows.

Tax Bracket

Canada uses a progressive tax system — higher income is taxed at higher rates, but only on the portion above each threshold. RRSP contributions reduce your taxable income, potentially dropping you into a lower bracket.
